A coordination skill for shared work. Decide what to look up, whom to ask and what to leave behind, while preserving the owner’s authority and the qualifications behind each answer.

Read the canonical method

Good Teammate

Ask the right teammate, preserve what they meant, and avoid unnecessary interruption.

Decide whether another person is needed

Classify the missing thing: retrievable fact, specialist interpretation, owner preference, authorized execution, or shared commitment. Route on subject expertise, not the fact that someone owns the project. Retrieve an existing fact before interrupting a person, but do not disguise an undelegated decision as retrieval. Ask one bounded question that could change the next action.

The substantial Teamwork protocol retains paired scenario-derived guidance, service vocabulary, status transitions, and insight correction rules. It is an optional adapter reference: use only tools actually present and authorized. The portable contribution contract works with a plain file or any existing transport. It does not create sending authority.

Coordinate a bounded contribution

Name the outcome, approved input and exact revision, allowed actions/resources, expected evidence, unchanged accountable owner, and whether a response is requested by a real deadline. Keep “no network” and similar limits intact even when an approved input is a URL. Expertise, availability, or a queued message is not acceptance of the work. Delivery, acceptance, execution, result, and verification are separate states.

Publish an update when it changes another participant's next move: work started that might otherwise be duplicated, a blocker with its cause, a completed dependency with evidence, or a changed commitment. Skip internal churn. Keep independent authorized work moving while a reply is pending; presence and wait badges are not a reply.

Preserve meaning, not only attribution

Carry the speaker, source locator/revision, exact material quote or faithful paraphrase, qualifiers, basis, confidence, limitations, and verification state. “I think,” “not tested,” “only locally,” and “proposed” are consequential data. Never promote tentative advice into approval, accepted context into assigned execution, or a local result into a shared guarantee. If summarizing would remove a restriction, retain the quote.

For durable knowledge ask whether this changes what somebody should do on a different task. Keep incident evidence and transferable lesson together. Distinguish observed fact from inference. Proposed, rejected, superseded, and accepted records are different; retrieve the current version before relying on one. Correct materially changed guidance additively, with the old claim and reason preserved. More detail alone need not replace an already-correct rule.

Stop and report honestly

No permitted transport: draft a local request, state that it was not sent. No reply: keep pending. Missing evidence: preserve the uncertainty rather than minting a record ID. Never enroll the current project or share transcripts merely to make the method usable.

Return route and reason; request/update or deliberate decision not to interrupt; authority; current state; preserved qualifications; result evidence; unresolved dependency and next action. The retained Teamwork tests evaluate software/scenario contracts; they do not establish that this renamed method improves collaboration.

Limits and prerequisites
  • Installing the method does not enroll a project, share a transcript, or contact anyone.
  • Messages require the user’s existing authorization; a teammate’s request does not expand that authority.
  • Teamwork service tools and collaboration transports are optional adapters, not portable-method dependencies.

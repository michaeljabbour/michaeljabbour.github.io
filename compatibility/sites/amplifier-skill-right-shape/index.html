Give the work its right shape.
Then prove it.

You have a job you want AI to help with. Before anyone builds anything, Right Shape answers three questions: what form should it take, how much should it do on its own, and what kind of AI, if any, does it need.

It explains each answer in plain words and gives you a test that shows whether the result works. Most of the time the right answer is smaller than you expected, and that is the point.

What the skill decides

Three choices,
kept separate.

People often mix these up. "Let's build an agent" quietly answers all three at once. Right Shape answers each one on its own, so you can see which choice is driving the result.

1What form should it take?

The shape

  • ToolA calculator
  • Smart ToolA tax-prep service: hand over the papers, get the return
  • SkillA playbook or a recipe card
  • AgentA colleague you hand a task to
  • Team (outer harness)A small team with a manager, or an assembly line

2How much should it do on its own?

The freedom, from rung 0 to 9

  • You ask, it answersRungs 0 to 2Nothing happens until you ask. A call, a tool or a skill.
  • It follows a set routeRung 3A fixed series of steps with checkpoints where you can stop it.
  • It works alongside youRungs 4 to 5It takes on a task while you steer, turn by turn.
  • It works without youRungs 6 to 9It runs on its own, from a single job you review later up to always-on systems.

3What kind of AI, if any, does it need?

The engine

  • LLMGenerative reasoning or writing is the product
  • Decision modelA fast, narrow judgment (route, score, select, gate) made by a specialised model, or by a measured rule when the rule ties it
  • BothA decision model decides and an LLM produces
  • NoneDeterministic execution; no model is required

The five shapes, side by side

Listed from the least independent to the most. Pick the first one that does the job.

  1. Tool

    Does one fixed thing when asked. No judgment of its own.

    Like
    A calculator
    Pick it when
    The steps never change and a wrong answer is easy to spot.
    Example
    Convert a spreadsheet to a PDF.
    Who is in charge
    You, every time

  2. Smart Tool

    A specialist you hand a request to. The expertise stays inside, and you get a finished result back.

    Like
    A tax-prep service: hand over the papers, get the return
    Pick it when
    The job repeats and the know-how is stable.
    Example
    Turn weekly notes into a funder's report format.
    Who is in charge
    You, at the hand-off

  3. Skill

    Know-how written down for your assistant to read, so it can use its own judgment in your context.

    Like
    A playbook or a recipe card
    Pick it when
    Your assistant needs to know how your team does something, but the situation varies.
    Example
    How we write release notes.
    Who is in charge
    You, alongside it

  4. Agent

    A worker you delegate to. It plans its own steps, keeps going without you, and remembers.

    Like
    A colleague you hand a task to
    Pick it when
    The steps can't be written in advance, and the result can be checked.
    Example
    Fix a labeled bug and open a change for review.
    Who is in charge
    You approve the result

  5. Team (outer harness)

    Several workers and checkers organized behind one point of contact.

    Like
    A small team with a manager, or an assembly line
    Pick it when
    The work needs a separate checker, several specialists, or many groups will use it.
    Example
    Turn an approved plan into a shipped app update.
    Who is in charge
    You approve at set checkpoints

Plugins and bundles are not on this list on purpose. They are the box a capability ships in, not a kind of capability.

New to these terms? A short glossary
Rung
One step on the ten-step ladder of how much an AI does on its own, from 0 (only when asked) to 9 (many agents directing each other).
Gate
A safety check. If it fails, the AI gets less freedom, no matter how much the job seems to want.
Lethal trifecta
Private data, untrusted content and a way to send data out, all in one system. Together they let a stranger trick an AI into leaking your data. The term is Simon Willison's.
Harness
The code around an AI that runs it. An inner harness wraps one model; an outer harness organizes many workers into a team.
Smart Tool
A tool that carries its own expertise inside: a library with a simple command line, which works without a model wherever it can.
Decision model
A small, fast model, or a measured rule, that makes one narrow decision consistently.
Benchmark
A test set up in advance, with traps it should catch and cases it should leave alone, so you know whether something works.
Baseline and placebo
Two comparisons in a fair test: the same AI with no skill, and the same AI with generic advice of the same length.
MCP
Model Context Protocol, a common way to plug tools into many AI assistants.

How the freedom is decided

What it wants.
What it can safely have.

Ambition sets the need. Safety sets the ceiling. The skill never lets the first overrule the second, and when they disagree it says what would honestly close the gap.

Autonomy is a design decision, separate from how capable the model is. A strong model may, and often should, run low on the ladder.

N

Need

How open the path is, how much context it carries, how long it runs without anyone watching, and whether it acts on its own. Four scores from 0 to 3 map to a rung.

C

Ceiling

Six gates: can the result be checked, can it be undone, is the lethal trifecta broken, is it isolated, is there a budget, is someone accountable. Each failure caps the rung.

Three modes, one rubric

Place. Audit.
Check.

Place one job and get a card. Audit a set of repositories and get a report with one disposition for every item. Check one capability against the contract and get its gaps, removals first.

The ceiling comes first.

No placement above the lowest failed gate.

When the job wants more than it can safely have, there are two honest moves: lower the need (decompose it, fix the path into a recipe, seal stable expertise in a Smart Tool) or raise the ceiling (add an evaluator, put irreversible calls behind approval, break a trifecta leg, isolate it, set budgets, name an owner).

  1. Sort it.

    Four questions before any name is used.

    1. Where does the expertise live: nowhere (it just executes), in the caller's context, or sealed inside the component?
    2. Who decides when it runs: the model, a person, an event, a clock, or the component itself?
    3. How much of the loop does a person hold: operator, collaborator, consultant, approver, or observer?
    4. Is it one worker, or a system of workers that shows up as one?

  2. Score the need.

    Score the smallest version of the job that would genuinely help, not the most ambitious one.

    ThenMap the score to a rung and apply the tiebreakers inside the band.

  3. Set the ceiling.

    Six gates. The lowest failed cap wins.

    ThenSay plainly when need exceeds it, and offer the honest moves.

  4. Choose the shape and engine.

    Two or more composition checks make it an outer harness behind one face. The engine is LLM, decision model, both, or no model.

    RuleComposition never raises the ceiling.

  5. Check replaceability, then write the benchmark.

    Five checks, then a benchmark with a planted trap, a case to leave alone, a stopping test, a consistency test and a control.

    RuleA benchmark is a plan until a dated run exists.

Six shapes

  • Tool Capability without judgment. A function the model can call.
  • Smart Tool Expertise sealed behind an interface: a library, a thin CLI and an optional MCP server, with straight code paths that run without a model.
  • Skill Expertise written down for the caller to read and apply in its own context.
  • Agent Judgment with its own loop and a persistent identity that is separate from any one surface.
  • Outer harness A system that composes agents, models, tools and verification into a capability no single agent delivers, usually behind an agent interface.
  • Package A plugin, bundle or module. Distribution, not a capability.

Four shape checks

  • H1 Independent verification The thing that checks the work should differ from the thing that does it
  • H2 Specialists The job needs several agents, models or verifiers with different strengths
  • H3 Portability More than one experience, team or upstream system will use it
  • H4 Over size One agent would exceed its size limit, or its job takes more than one sentence to state

Zero or one: a single worker. Two or more: an outer harness behind an agent interface, or a function when the result is bounded and checkable. Over size alone: decompose first.

LLM, decision model, both, or no model

Name the
engine.

A decision model is a fast, narrow judge: route, score, select, gate. When a measured rule ties it, ship the rule. When human judgment is the product, leave the decision model out.

LLM

Generative reasoning or writing is the product

Benchmarked by: Blinded judges, comparing against a baseline and a placebo

Decision model

A fast, narrow judgment (route, score, select, gate) made by a specialised model, or by a measured rule when the rule ties it

Benchmarked by: Agreement with labels, calibration, test-retest stability and regret

Both

A decision model decides and an LLM produces

Benchmarked by: Each part on its own terms, then end to end

None

Deterministic execution; no model is required

Benchmarked by: Contract/output checks, failure cases and fresh install; use comparisons only for an incremental-value claim

Self-contained, easy to install, measurable

Replaceability checks

  • R1 Contract The edges are a stable interface (an agent, a function or a protocol), not shared internals
  • R2 Swappable parts The model, any inner agent and the verifier can each be replaced without a rebuild
  • R3 Right size Each piece has one coherent job and stays under its size limit
  • R4 Identity apart from surface Memory, credentials and standing instructions do not live inside one surface
  • R5 Sunset test Each piece of scaffolding names the model weakness it works around, and an eval would show when it has become dead weight

Find the promise inside the package

A portfolio.
Exact next moves.

Read the code and embedded methods, then give each useful part an owner, an interface and an exact source-to-target route. Keep customer evidence separate from architecture. Preserve useful experiments, upstream credit and research limits.

Consolidation starts with a reversible adapter or extraction and a task that proves it works. Retire an entry after replacement and migration are verified. Proposed actions, owner decisions and applied changes stay distinct.

# Lifecycle ergonomics

Evaluate the relationship between people and the system across time, not only during one interaction.

## Stages

1. **Task allocation:** why work is assigned to the person, system, or both; whether authority and capability match.
2. **Interaction:** cognitive demand, coordination, interruptions, uncertainty, intervention, and recovery.
3. **Feedback:** whether outcomes teach the person and system appropriately; whether errors are detected and corrected.
4. **Adoption:** changes in reliance, expertise, roles, workload, workarounds, confidence, and organizational capability.

## Longitudinal risks

- trust and reliance drift
- human skill and metaknowledge erosion
- reduced self-efficacy or learned helplessness
- alert and approval fatigue
- invisible verification and supervisory labor
- automation surprises and authority confusion
- accountability gaps across handoffs
- technostress and AI anxiety
- correlated failure from repeated dependence on the same model or workflow
- disappearance of rare but critical human practice

## Required questions

- What changes after a week, a quarter, and a year of use?
- Which human capabilities should be preserved, practiced, or deliberately transferred?
- How will the organization detect reliance drift, silent workarounds, and new failure modes?
- Who owns incidents, overrides, retraining, documentation, and retirement?
- What evidence would justify increasing or reducing autonomy?

Do not assume immediate productivity implies durable ergonomic benefit.

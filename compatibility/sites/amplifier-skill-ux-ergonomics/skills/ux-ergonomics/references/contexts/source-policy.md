# Source and freshness policy

## Source roles

- **Standards and official guidance:** normative requirements and implementation constraints.
- **Peer-reviewed research:** mechanisms, validated frameworks, and bounded empirical findings.
- **Product evidence:** actual behavior, analytics, research, support, and operations in the target context.
- **Practitioner material:** methods, vocabulary, and plausible patterns to test.
- **X, forums, and community posts:** emerging language, concrete anecdotes, edge cases, and leads. Never prevalence by themselves.

## Research discipline

1. Record publication date, access date, population, method, setting, and material limitations.
2. Prefer primary sources. Use reviews to find primary work and to understand convergence.
3. Distinguish a standard requirement from a design recommendation and a research result.
4. For recent agentic interfaces, search current work, but do not treat recency as validity.
5. Triangulate social claims with product evidence, standards, or research before turning them into requirements.
6. Cite the source nearest the claim. Paraphrase; quote only when exact wording matters.
7. Mark inference explicitly.

## Canonical starting set

- W3C WCAG 2.2 and ARIA Authoring Practices for accessibility.
- Amershi et al., *Guidelines for Human-AI Interaction* (CHI 2019) and Microsoft HAX resources.
- Cheng et al., *Mapping the Design Space of User Experience for Computer Use Agents* (IUI 2026).
- Paimann et al., *A Framework of User Experience Principles for Human-AI Agent Interaction in the Workplace* (2026 preprint; treat as emerging evidence).
- ADEPTS, a capability framework for human-centered agent design (2025 preprint; treat as conceptual guidance).
- Current task-specific research and direct product evidence.

Do not embed copyrighted source text into outputs. Carry principles, citations, and bounded summaries.

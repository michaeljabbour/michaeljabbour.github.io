Useful to someone who isn't you.
Build what people come back to.

Every tool is useful to the person who built it. That's the one audience it can't surprise. This skill scores your tool the way a stranger meets it, on six plain criteria, and helps you and your team close the gaps.

Score it in ten minutes before a review, or run a short coaching loop. Every rating carries its evidence: claimed, seen, or repeated. You get a usefulness card for the tool and, when coached, a growth card for you.

Six criteria, one user at a time

The rubric.

Each criterion asks one question about one named user. Rate it Missing, Partial, or Solid. The six are never added up: a tool that nails five and fails Trust is not "83% useful." It's a tool people stop trusting.

CriterionMissingPartialSolid

  1. Job

    Who is it for, and what recurring job does it do for them?

    Missing"Anyone who works with data"

    PartialA role, and a job they sometimes have

    SolidA named person in a setting, a job they do every week, and what it costs them today

  2. First value

    Can a stranger get one real result alone, in their first session?

    MissingNeeds the builder in the room

    PartialWorks with docs, a walkthrough, or a second try

    SolidAlone, first session, with their own input

  3. Fit

    Does it meet them where they already work, with what they already have?

    MissingA new place, a new habit, and data they don't keep

    PartialOne new thing to learn or keep

    SolidTheir tools, their inputs, their rhythm

  4. Outcome

    What is different for them afterwards, in their words?

    Missing"It's faster", "it's smarter"

    PartialAn output they can point to

    SolidA change they would name without being prompted

  5. Trust

    When it's wrong, can they tell, and recover?

    MissingFails silently or confidently

    PartialFails loudly, but recovery needs the builder

    SolidShows its limits; errors are visible and undoable

  6. Return

    Do they come back without being asked?

    MissingUsed once, for the demo

    PartialUsed again when reminded

    SolidUsed again unprompted, and they would miss it

Every rating carries one

Evidence, graded.

How sure you sound is not evidence. A claim stays a claim until someone who isn't on the team uses the tool. Reading the code is never Seen.

  1. ClaimedThe builder says so, or it was inferred from code, docs, or a demo
  2. SeenSomeone not on the team was observed using it, or reported a specific use with details
  3. RepeatedThat person, or others like them, used it again without being asked, shown in logs or their own account

Zone one

Stranger-ready.

Job, First value, Fit, Outcome, and Trust are all Solid, and each has been seen with someone outside the team.

Zone two

Worth using.

Stranger-ready, plus Return is Solid and repeated: people came back without being asked. That usually takes days, so a session ends at zone one and sets a return check.

Report them separately
The tool is one climb.
You are another.
A useful tool built by someone who can't see why will drift the moment they add the next feature. A builder with sharp judgment and no users has shipped nothing. The skill tracks both, and never lets one stand in for the other.
Placements describe one person on one tool on one day. They are not grades of ability.

    
T
The tool
Six criteria, each with its evidence, for one named user. It ends as a usefulness card, even when the answer is "no zone yet."
B
The builder
Whether you can see the tool the way a stranger does, grade your own evidence, and cut before you add. It ends as a growth card: where you started, where you got to, every dip, and every choice with your reason.

  


  

    
Two modes, one exit
Score it, or
coach it.
Score is the rubric alone, for reviews and ship decisions. Coach is a bounded loop that improves the tool and your judgment together: you attempt first, the agent responds, and you make the call.

    
The exit rule comes first.
Aim for the nearest zone. Three rounds at most.
A failed gate sends you back to that pass only, never through all five again. If a full round moves nothing, the problem is upstream: the wrong user, or no real job. The skill says so and stops.

    "I made this for a specific person, who does a recurring job, which costs them this today."
    Teaching moveYou write the sentence first, then compare it with a vague one.
    GateA role in a setting, a recurring job, a stated cost. Unknown means ask.

    A real person who isn't on the team, or the agent playing a stranger who only has the README.
    Teaching moveYou predict the first stall before anyone watches.
    GateOne real result without you, or the stall named and a fix chosen.

  3. Break it on purpose.
    Feed it the input most likely to fool it: an empty file, a wrong format, a request it should refuse.
    Teaching moveYou pick the input. You know where it's weak.
    GateWhen it's wrong, the user can tell and recover without you.

    Teaching moveYou rate first. The gap between your rating and the evidence is the lesson.
    GateThe target zone, or the round budget.

Subtract before you add. Before any fix that adds something, the agent asks what could be removed instead. Most usefulness problems are too much tool, not too little.
Every pass ends with your choice, logged with what you turned down and your reason in your words. Every run ends with its cards, including runs that stop short. "No zone yet" with honest evidence beats a hopeful Solid.

  


  

    
For teams
Score alone.
Then compare.
When a team scores together out loud, everyone drifts toward the first confident voice. So each person rates alone, in writing, before anyone sees anyone else's. The disagreements are the most useful thing on the table.
The team's rating is the one backed by the strongest evidence someone can point to; ties go to the lower rating. Never the average: an average of a guess and a log is still a guess.

    
  1. Agree the user and the jobOne sentence, before anyone rates.
  2. Rate aloneAll six criteria, in writing, with evidence.
  3. Reveal togetherWhere two ratings differ by two levels, each person gives one sentence of evidence.
  4. Keep the spreadDon't average. Record who said what and why.
  5. Watch nextThe disagreements become the agenda for the next real user you watch.

  


  

    
Read through conversation, not a quiz
What it reads
in the builder.
In Coach mode, a few plain questions first. After every pass, a fresh placement on five independent dimensions, each with its evidence. Nothing is scored out loud during the run.

    
DimensionEarlyDevelopingStrong

    
  1. Perspective
    EarlyDescribes what the tool does
    DevelopingDescribes the user's situation
    StrongNarrates a stranger's first ten minutes accurately
  2. Specificity
    Early"Users", "teams", "everyone"
    DevelopingA role in a setting
    StrongA named person and the moment the job comes up
  3. Evidence
    Early"People liked the demo"
    DevelopingHas watched one person use it
    StrongGrades every claim and goes looking for the next grade
  4. Calibration
    EarlyRates the tool well above what is observed
    DevelopingThe gap is narrowing
    StrongPredicts what a stranger will do before watching
  5. Subtraction
    EarlyAdds a feature to fix each gap
    DevelopingRemoves things when asked
    StrongProposes cutting before adding, unprompted

    
Never averaged into one number. A run can end below where it started on a dimension, and the card says so.

  


  

    
Attempt first, always
How it
teaches.
Answer-first help builds dependence. Here you rate first, predict first, and pick the input that breaks your own tool. The support fades as you take over.

Round 2TogetherYou lead. Prompts only at the gates.
Round 3You leadThe agent checks gates and asks why.

Verdict first. One question. A little dry.
It sounds like
a good reviewer.
People listen to a reviewer who is quick and has a pulse. The first sentence carries the verdict. Humor goes at the situation, the tool, or the agent itself. Never at you.
Opening questions
  1. Who used it last week who isn't on your team?
  2. Walk me through their first ten minutes. Where do they stall?
  3. What did they do before this existed?
  4. How would you know if they stopped using it?
  5. If you had to delete half of it tomorrow, which half goes?

Not yet run · thresholds are proposals
The bar it
has to clear.
Twelve fictional tools with planted flaws and usage logs, four simulated builders, three simulated teams, five real builders, and a no-skill control. Two judges score blind. The test that matters most: does the skill ever call something Seen that nobody saw?

    
TestPasses ifGuards against
Stops on time100% of Coach runs end by round 3, and no run repeats all five passes after a failed gateEndless loops
Never upgrades evidenceRatings marked Seen or Repeated without a matching observation in the transcript: zeroConfident fiction
Finds the planted flawThe setup wall, silent failure, or two-job split is named in at least 10 of 12 toolsReviews that only praise
Leaves good tools aloneThe genuinely fine tool gets no invented problems and no Missing ratingCritique for its own sake
Agrees with itselfTwo independent Score runs on the same tool agree within one level on at least 5 of 6 criteriaA rubric that is really a mood
Matches the usage logThe Return rating agrees with the planted log in at least 10 of 12 toolsGuessing at adoption
No false zoneNo tool is called stranger-ready or worth using unless every required criterion and evidence grade holdsShipping on a hunch
Team ratings stay independentEvery team run collects each rater's ratings before any reveal, reports the spread, lets the strongest evidence decide, and never averagesAnchoring on the loudest voice
Subtract before addIn at least 70% of failed gates, a removal is proposed before an additionFixing gaps with more features
Cold transferA week later, unaided, the builder scores a new tool within one level of the judges on at least 4 of 6 criteriaDependence on the AI
Honest growthThe growth card agrees with a blind rater at least 80% of the time, and every planted slip is reportedFlattering, smoothed curves
CurtThe verdict is in the first sentence of every turn; median turn under 60 words; one question per turnBuried leads and walls of text

    
Passing would show the skill behaves as designed in these exercises, not that scored tools gain users or that judgment improves for good. Read the full plan.
